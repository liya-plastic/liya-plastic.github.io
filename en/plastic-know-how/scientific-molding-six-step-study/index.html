Scientific molding: six studies that turn settings into a process

Viscosity, cavity balance, pressure drop, process window, gate seal, and cooling: six studies that replace knob-turning with measured evidence.

A machine setup is not yet a process. If the reason behind each value is unknown, a resin lot change, season, machine transfer, or minor mold condition change can push the result out of control with no clear recovery path.

Scientific molding separates the process into physical studies that can be measured, documented, and repeated. Liya's knowledge base follows this sequence: viscosity, cavity balance, pressure drop, process window, gate seal, and cooling time. The order matters because each later study depends on a stable result from the earlier ones.

Key takeaways

  • The goal is not one perfect setting; it is a stable operating window with known limits.
  • Fill, pack, and cooling are studied separately so one stage does not hide the weakness of another.
  • Part weight, pressure, fill time, cavity balance, dimensions, and defects become the evidence for the setup sheet.
  • Pack pressure should not be a fixed percentage copied from injection pressure; define it from measured part response and safe limits.

The six studies in order

StepStudyMethod and output
1Viscosity / rheology curveVary fill speed while holding other conditions; compare relative viscosity using peak pressure × fill time to identify a stable shear region
2Cavity balanceMake controlled short shots and weigh each cavity; quantify runner/gate imbalance before pack hides it
3Pressure-drop studyMeasure the pressure required to advance flow through nozzle, runner, gate, and part; locate the largest restriction and verify machine headroom
4Process window / DOEVary selected factors and map the region that meets fill, flash, appearance, and dimension requirements
5Gate-seal studyIncrease hold time in steps and weigh parts; when weight no longer rises, the gate is no longer transmitting pack
6Cooling-time studyIncrease cooling and measure key dimensions/shape until ejection is stable; identify the shortest verified cooling time

Why the sequence matters

Cavity balance is checked before pack pressure can compensate for an imbalanced runner. Pressure drop is studied before choosing a window so the machine is not operating at the edge of available pressure. The process window is established before gate seal because hold time has meaning only inside a defined fill and temperature condition.

Cooling comes last because ejection stability and final dimensions depend on the preceding fill and pack decisions. Changing early-stage conditions after a cooling study invalidates part of the evidence and should trigger a targeted recheck.

What scientific molding deliberately avoids

  • Choosing fill speed from sound or appearance alone without a viscosity study.
  • Using pack pressure to cover an unbalanced multi-cavity runner.
  • Setting hold pressure to a fixed 25%, 50%, or 75% of injection pressure without part-response data.
  • Calling one defect-free sample a process window.
  • Reducing cooling until parts barely release, without checking delayed dimension and shape recovery.

The deliverables are more important than the graphs

Each study should leave a short, auditable record: test conditions, raw measurements, selected value, acceptable range, rejection criteria, and the reason for the decision. The final setup sheet should distinguish machine settings from process outputs such as fill time, peak pressure, cushion, part weight, and cavity balance.

That distinction makes machine transfer and troubleshooting more robust. A different machine may need different setpoints to reproduce the same process outputs; copying screen values alone does not guarantee the same plastic behavior in the cavity.

Scientific molding does not remove experience. It converts experience into hypotheses that can be tested, shared, and improved.

FAQ

What are the six scientific molding studies?

Viscosity curve, cavity balance, pressure drop, process window/DOE, gate seal, and cooling-time studies, performed in that order.

Why is gate seal studied after the process window?

Hold time is meaningful only after fill speed, pressure behavior, temperature, and an acceptable operating region are established. Otherwise the result changes with the unverified base condition.
