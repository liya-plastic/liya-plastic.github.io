Short shot, flash, sink, or warp? A D1–D4 diagnostic guide

Four defects can point in opposite process directions. Start from the symptom and physics before turning pressure, speed, temperature, or clamp force.

The fastest adjustment is not always the right adjustment. A short shot and flash can sit on opposite sides of V/P transfer; a sink mark and warpage may both involve shrinkage but demand different checks. Turning several settings at once destroys the evidence needed to find the actual cause.

Liya's defect index starts with the visible symptom, then separates material, mold, machine, and parameter causes. The sequence below is a triage order—not permission to skip part design, mold condition, or measurement.

Key takeaways

  • Short shot means the melt cannot reach or fill the final region; flash means cavity pressure or sealing exceeded what the mold/clamp could contain.
  • Sink is a local packing and thick-section problem; warp is a non-uniform shrinkage and stress problem across the part.
  • Change one variable family at a time and record part weight, fill pattern, cavity behavior, and dimensions.
  • If a defect stays at the same physical location across settings, inspect geometry, venting, gate, cooling, and mold wear before forcing the process harder.

D1–D4 symptom-to-action map

DefectWhat you seeFirst investigation order
D1 Short shotMissing material at end of fill, thin wall, deep rib, or distant cavityVerify V/P transfer is not early → confirm available pressure/speed → check melt/mold temperature, venting, gate and runner restriction
D2 FlashThin film at parting line, ejector, slide, or insert clearanceCheck late V/P transfer or overpacking → reduce unsafe pack pressure → verify real clamp force, mold parallelism, sealing surfaces and vent depth
D3 Sink markLocal depression opposite a rib, boss, or thick massConfirm pack pressure/time reaches gate seal → verify cushion → inspect thick geometry, gate freeze and local mold temperature
D4 WarpageBowing, corner lift, twist, or an unstable flat planeCompare core/cavity cooling → inspect packing gradient and fiber orientation → extend cooling and review wall/gate symmetry

Short shot and flash may be opposite signals

An early V/P transfer can leave velocity-controlled filling before the cavity is sufficiently full, producing a short shot. A late transfer can convert the remaining melt momentum into a pressure spike and push material through the parting line as flash. This is why the transfer position, fill time, peak pressure, and short-shot progression should be reviewed together.

Do not automatically solve short shot with more temperature or pressure. A trapped-gas region, frozen gate, restricted runner, insufficient shot capacity, or wrong flow grade can produce the same visible symptom. For flash, more clamp force cannot repair a damaged seal-off or an over-deep vent.

Sink and warp share shrinkage—but not the same geometry

Sink is usually local. A thick core keeps shrinking after the surface has stiffened, and insufficient packing compensation pulls the skin inward. Warp is a change in overall shape caused by different shrinkage across the part—from cooling imbalance, asymmetric geometry, orientation, crystallization, glass fiber direction, or uneven packing.

A parameter change can hide the symptom without eliminating its source. Increasing pack pressure may improve a sink mark but increase residual stress or flash elsewhere. Extending cooling may hold shape at ejection but cannot fully correct an asymmetric water circuit or strongly directional fiber orientation.

A disciplined troubleshooting record

  • Photograph the defect and mark its exact mold location and cavity number.
  • Record resin lot, drying state, machine, mold, cycle, cushion, part weight, and critical dimensions.
  • Separate fill from pack by making controlled short shots when safe and appropriate.
  • Change one variable or one linked variable family at a time.
  • Return the verified condition to a controlled setup sheet; do not leave the fix as operator memory.
Troubleshooting is a diagnosis loop. A stable process comes from identifying the physical cause, not from finding one setting that makes today's sample look acceptable.

FAQ

What is the first adjustment for an injection molding short shot?

First verify that V/P transfer is not occurring too early. Then confirm available pressure, speed, melt condition, venting, and flow restrictions instead of raising every setting together.

What is the difference between sink marks and warpage?

Sink is a local surface depression from shrinkage in a thick or under-packed region. Warpage is an overall shape change caused by non-uniform shrinkage, cooling, orientation, or stress.
